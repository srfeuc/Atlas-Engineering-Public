# Risk as a Living Register (one module)

<!-- provenance -->
> **Atlas Academy — Concepts.** A "why it works" module (`Academy/README` 4-part format), in the ⭐ [golden Concept shape](./Encryption-and-PKI-in-Atlas.md). Every claim points at a **real Atlas artifact** — a real accepted risk with real triggers, a real "we can't see" gap, a real fallback that was nearly deleted. The *process, register, and BIA* live in [`POL-0012` Risk Management](../../00-Atlas-Foundation/Policies/POL-0012-Risk-Management.md); this page explains the **reasoning** so the discipline is understood, not just filled in. One home per fact (`POL-0004`).

> **The gap this closes ([`POL-0012`](../../00-Atlas-Foundation/Policies/POL-0012-Risk-Management.md)):** the policy carries the whole apparatus — identification, qualitative + quantitative analysis, treatment strategies, a register, a BIA — but a rule is a *what*. This is the *why*: why an accepted risk is a decision rather than a shrug, why "no evidence" is not the same as "no problem," and why a risk register is only worth keeping if it is *living*.

## The Concept

Every estate runs on risks it has decided to live with. The discipline isn't avoiding them — it's making each one a **documented decision with an owner, a treatment, and a trigger**, so the whole picture is visible at once instead of scattered across people's memories. Four ideas make that real.

**Accept is a decision, not a shrug.** The five treatments — *mitigate* (add a control), *transfer* (insurance, a vendor SLA), *accept* (take it knowingly), *avoid* (don't do the thing), plus temporary *exceptions* and standing *exemptions* — are all legitimate, but **acceptance is the one people get wrong**, because they accept a risk and then forget they did. A real acceptance names the reversal triggers and the review date up front. *"An accepted risk with no review trigger is not an accepted risk — it is a forgotten one."* Accept ≠ ignore.

**The register is what makes it *management* instead of a pile of decisions.** Any single risk can live in the decision that accepted it; what a register adds is the *whole board in one view* — every risk with a likelihood, an impact, an owner, a treatment, a residual rating, a **KRI** (a key risk indicator with a threshold that re-rates it when crossed), and a review trigger. Risk without a register is a set of good calls nobody can see together; the register is the report.

**"No evidence" is not "no problem" — and a gap isn't closed until the fix is *running*.** The most honest and most dangerous line in risk work is admitting what you can't see. A lab with no monitoring can say "no evidence of compromise," but that means *"we cannot see," not "we looked"* — which is itself the top risk, because it hides all the others. And a risk that a *design* closes is not closed while the mitigation is only designed: a control that exists on paper but isn't device-verified running is an open risk wearing a closed label (**gap analysis** as a standing tool — the difference between where a system *is* and where the design *says* it should be, read as a risk register).

**A control you've never needed looks exactly like a control you don't need.** This is the quiet failure mode that connects risk to everything else: the fallback nobody has used, the log nobody reads, the recovery path that's been idle for months — each is indistinguishable from dead weight, so each gets pruned in good faith, and the risk it silently covered comes back invisible. Reading an *expected quiet result* (an idle interface, a clean scan, a cipher-0 probe against a closed channel) as *evidence of safety* is the same mistake in a different hat.

Sitting under all four: a lab has a **split risk appetite** — expansionary toward *self-inflicted, contained* risk (it exists to break things and learn) and conservative toward the crown jewels and the production line (where availability is sacred) — and quantitative math (SLE, ARO, ALE) earns its place as a **tie-breaker and a communication tool**, not false precision.

## The Atlas Example (real artifacts)

**The textbook acceptance ([`ADR-0009`](../../00-Atlas-Foundation/Decisions/ADR-0009-Intermediate-CA-Not-Treated-as-Compromised.md)).** After a 15-hour key+passphrase convergence, the estate assessed the blast radius (a home-lab CA; low payoff), chose to **accept** rather than do the expensive replacement now — and did the part most people skip: named **explicit reversal triggers** (a cert appears that the CA didn't issue; any evidence of workstation compromise; the lab is ever exposed or hosts anything of value) and a **scheduled review** (the 2027 renewal). That ADR is where `POL-0012`'s defining rule comes from: *"an accepted risk with no review trigger is a forgotten one."* Its `index.txt`-as-a-control finding is the same idea applied to detection — *"a compromise-detection control whose baseline is wrong fails loud and wrong"* ([`CM-0032`](../../Labs/Lab-01-Mikrotik-Core/Change-Management/CM-0032-CA-Database-Has-No-Record-of-Two-Certificates.md)).

**The register, populated with real risks (`POL-0012` Part D).** Not a template — eleven real Atlas risks, each with an owner, a KRI threshold, and a trigger: both CA+vault backups in one room (R-01, mitigate → off-site copy), the possibly-compromised intermediate CA (R-02, accept + triggers), the unpatchable OT box (R-04, mitigate by segmentation — *can't* avoid, the line needs it), FGT01's broad egress accepted **until redundancy exists** ([`ADR-0005`](../../00-Atlas-Foundation/Decisions/ADR-0005-FGT01-Firewall-Policy-Scope-Deferred.md), an *exception* with an expiry), and — bluntly — **R-11: "No monitoring — we cannot see,"** rated High because it's the meta-risk hiding the rest. The estate's live instruments are the [Review-Flag-Register](../../Labs/Lab-02-Cisco-Core/Review-Flag-Register.md) (the open-flag board) and the [Lab-01→Lab-02 gap map](../Lab-01-to-Lab-02-Reconciliation-and-Gap-Map.md) (which design gaps the new build closes, and which mitigations aren't *running* yet). A worked quantitative example (R-01: ALE ≈ $160/yr and catastrophic vs a $55 control) exists precisely to show the honest use of the math — it confirms the qualitative "High," it doesn't manufacture false precision.

**The split appetite, on real targets (`POL-0012` Part B).** [`ADR-0011`](../../00-Atlas-Foundation/Decisions/ADR-0011-Game-Day-Unannounced-Failure-Drills.md) runs *unannounced failure drills on purpose* — that's the expansionary appetite toward contained, self-inflicted risk — while the `301` company is conservative for Restricted data and the OT line ([`305`](../../00-Atlas-Foundation/Company-Profile/305-Atlas-Industrial-Security-Requirements.md): a control that risks stopping production is *itself* a risk). Transfer is real too: cyber-insurance ([Third-Party-Risk-Management](../../00-Atlas-Foundation/Security-Program/Third-Party-Risk-Management.md)), whose attestation depends on the register being honest.

## What Went Wrong (real troubleshooting history — the best teacher)

- **The risk accepted with no home ([`ADR-0009`](../../00-Atlas-Foundation/Decisions/ADR-0009-Intermediate-CA-Not-Treated-as-Compromised.md)).** The convergence that created the risk happened because a backup procedure had a create step and no destroy step — *"the exposure was not created by a mistake; it was created by a procedure with a missing step."* The deeper lesson is what "acceptable" rested on: the payoff was low, *"not that the lab is clean, because the lab cannot currently tell whether it is clean."* An acceptance built on "we can't see" has to say so, and attach the trigger (Book 5 monitoring) that would let a future version of the decision be based on observation instead of inference.
- **"Retire it, it's inactive" ([`ADR-0013`](../../00-Atlas-Foundation/Decisions/ADR-0013-Retire-bridgeLocal-Recovery-Network.md)).** A routine-housekeeping proposal to retire an "unused" network turned out to target the admin recovery net two documents forbade removing — its ports read `INACTIVE` only because no cable was plugged in, *the correct resting state for a fallback.* The pattern it named is the core risk-blindness: *"a control that has never been needed looks identical to a control that is not needed,"* and *"every safety net that worked was one that failed loud"* — the quiet ones just haven't been tested yet. Retiring it would have removed a mitigation while the risk it covered was still live.
- **The deferral done honestly ([`ADR-0017`](../../00-Atlas-Foundation/Decisions/ADR-0017-Defer-CM-0012-CMOS-Battery.md)).** PVE01's out-of-band path is blocked by a dead CMOS battery; rather than tick "OOB present," the risk was **accepted and deferred in the open** with the unblock path named — *"a tick you did not earn is a lie."* A deferral you wrote down is risk management; a silent one is a forgotten risk.
- **The gap that a design "closed" but the build hadn't (the gap-map, #37).** The reconciliation between the old lab and the new design closes real weaknesses — but a *closed design gap with an unbuilt mitigation is still an open risk* (`POL-0001`: a gap is closed only when the fix is device-verified running). Marking it "designed-only-until-built" is the difference between a risk register and a wish list.

## How to Explain This in an Interview

*"To me, risk management isn't avoiding risk — it's making every risk a decision with an owner, a treatment, and a trigger, all in one register you can actually see. The part people get wrong is acceptance: they accept a risk and then forget they did. A real acceptance names what would reverse it and when you'll review it — in my lab I have a CA risk I accepted after a key exposure, but only with explicit triggers like 'a certificate appears that we didn't issue,' and a scheduled replacement. The honest core is admitting what you can't see: with no monitoring, 'no evidence of compromise' means 'we didn't look,' not 'we're clean' — so that blindness is itself the top risk, because it hides the others. And a risk a design closes isn't actually closed until the fix is running and verified, not just drawn. The pattern I watch for is that a control you've never needed looks exactly like one you don't need — the idle recovery path, the log nobody reads — so it gets deleted in good faith and the risk comes back invisible. I do use the quantitative math, SLE and ALE, but as a tie-breaker and a way to communicate, not to fake precision a home lab doesn't have."*
